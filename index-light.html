The Heart of the Reformation

The Five Solas

Five Latin phrases that crystallize the theological convictions of the Reformation — the doctrines for which men and women surrendered their lives rather than compromise Scripture.
✦ Click any sola to open its book ✦

✟
Sola Scriptura Scripture Alone

The Holy Bible is the only infallible rule for faith and life — supreme over councils, popes, and tradition.

✟
Sola Fide Faith Alone

Sinners are justified before God through faith alone — not sacraments, merits, or penances.

✟
Sola Gratia Grace Alone

Salvation is entirely the sovereign gift of God's grace, from election to glorification.

✟
Solus Christus Christ Alone

Jesus Christ, crucified and risen, is the sole mediator between God and mankind.

✟
Soli Deo Gloria Glory to God Alone

All things — creation, redemption, history itself — exist for the glory of God alone.

Soldiers of Grace

The Ten Great Puritans

Men who fought for a purer church, suffered exile and prison for their convictions, and left behind a treasury of pastoral theology that still feeds the soul five centuries on. Hover over each portrait to read their story.

I
John Owen 1616 – 1683
Hover to Explore
✟
John Owen The Prince of the Puritans · Oxford · England

Called the Prince of Puritans, Owen served as Vice-Chancellor of Oxford under Cromwell, championed Congregationalism, and wrote over 80 volumes of theology. His works on sin, the Holy Spirit, and Christ remain unequalled in depth and pastoral warmth.

"He who has slight thoughts of sin never had great thoughts of God."
✦ Read Biography
II
Richard Sibbes 1577 – 1635
Hover to Explore
✟
Richard Sibbes The Sweet Dropper · Cambridge · England

Known as "The Sweet Dropper" for his tender, comforting preaching, Sibbes revived Cambridge with his lectures at Holy Trinity. His Bruised Reed converted many and deeply influenced Puritanism's pastoral warmth and evangelical tone.

"God loves with a love of compassion; he pities our infirmities."
✦ Read Biography
III
Thomas Watson 1620 – 1686
Hover to Explore
✟
Thomas Watson Westminster Divine · London · England

A Westminster Assembly participant ejected in 1662, Watson's A Body of Practical Divinity remains the most accessible exposition of the Westminster Shorter Catechism. His vivid, aphoristic style makes deep theology instantly graspable.

"Thankfulness is the tune of heaven."
✦ Read Biography
IV
John Bunyan 1628 – 1688
Hover to Explore
✟
John Bunyan Tinker-Preacher · Bedford · England

A tinker who became the greatest Puritan storyteller. Imprisoned twelve years for unlicensed preaching, Bunyan wrote The Pilgrim's Progress — the most widely read book after the Bible. His courage and creativity remain an unmatched legacy.

"You have not lived today until you have done something for someone who can never repay you."
✦ Read Biography
V
William Perkins 1558 – 1602
Hover to Explore
✟
William Perkins Father of Puritanism · Cambridge · England

Converted from a life of drunkenness at Cambridge, Perkins became the foundational architect of Puritan theology. His Art of Prophesying shaped a generation of preachers; his complete works now run to ten volumes from Reformation Heritage Books.

"The Word of God alone is the foundation of all learning."
✦ Read Biography
VI
Thomas Brooks 1608 – 1680
Hover to Explore
✟
Thomas Brooks London Preacher · England

Beloved London preacher and prolific writer, Brooks excelled in diagnosing the devices of Satan and prescribing gospel remedies. His Precious Remedies Against Satan's Devices is arguably the most practically useful book the Puritans ever wrote.

"Satan promises the best but pays with the worst."
✦ Read Biography
VII
Samuel Rutherford 1600 – 1661
Hover to Explore
✟
Samuel Rutherford Scottish Covenanter · St Andrews · Scotland

Exiled to Aberdeen for preaching, Rutherford wrote letters from banishment that became among the most treasured spiritual correspondence in Christian history. C.H. Spurgeon said reading his letters felt like walking through a garden of spices.

"The cross of Christ is the sweetest burden that ever I bore."
✦ Read Biography
VIII
John Flavel 1628 – 1691
Hover to Explore
✟
John Flavel Dartmouth Minister · Devon · England

Twice ejected from his Dartmouth parish, Flavel preached in fields and ships and left six volumes of extraordinary pastoral theology. The Mystery of Providence remains Tim Challies' top Puritan Paperback recommendation for its life-changing perspective on God's sovereignty in ordinary events.

"The deeper we see into our own hearts, the higher we see God."
✦ Explore Works
IX
Richard Baxter 1615 – 1691
Hover to Explore
✟
Richard Baxter The Reformed Pastor · Kidderminster · England

Despite lifelong illness, Baxter transformed Kidderminster from irreligion to a model of gospel-saturated community. The Reformed Pastor remains the gold standard of pastoral theology, and The Saints' Everlasting Rest his meditation on heaven written while ill on horseback.

"Preach as a dying man to dying men."
✦ Explore Works
X
Jeremiah Burroughs 1599 – 1646
Hover to Explore
✟
Jeremiah Burroughs Westminster Independent · London · England

One of the most popular preachers of his age, Burroughs was called "the reconciler" for his irenic spirit amid Puritan controversies. His Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment is among the most personally transformative books the Puritans produced — a masterwork on Philippians 4:11.

"Contentment is a sweet, inward, quiet gracious frame of spirit, freely submitting to God's disposal."

Standards of Faith

Confessions & Catechisms

The great confessional documents of the Reformation — summaries of biblical doctrine forged in the crucible of controversy, persecution, and profound theological inquiry.

1530

Augsburg Confession

Lutheran

The primary Lutheran confessional document, presented to Emperor Charles V. Authored principally by Philip Melanchthon, it defined Lutheran doctrine against Rome.

 1563

Heidelberg Catechism

Reformed / Continental

One of the most beloved catechisms of the Reformation, opening with the question: “What is your only comfort in life and death?”

 1619

Canons of Dort

Reformed / TULIP

The Synod of Dort answered Arminius with the five doctrines of grace: Total Depravity, Unconditional Election, Definite Atonement, Irresistible Grace, Perseverance of the Saints.

 1646

Westminster Confession

Reformed / Presbyterian

The most thorough expression of Calvinist theology ever produced. Adopted by the Church of Scotland, PCA, OPC, and confessional Presbyterians worldwide.

 1647

Westminster Shorter Catechism

Reformed / Presbyterian

Opens with the immortal question: “What is the chief end of man?” — “To glorify God and enjoy him forever.” A masterwork memorized by generations of Reformed Christians.

 1689

London Baptist Confession

Reformed / Baptist

The definitive confession of Reformed Baptists, closely paralleling the Westminster Confession while insisting on believer's baptism by immersion.
